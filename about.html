About OmniTree

OmniTree is a start page: the first page you see when you open your browser. It puts the things you check every day side by side in one tab, so you stop opening the same ten sites every morning.

What you see on it

The page is made of boxes. Each box shows one thing, and you choose which boxes you want:

NewsHeadlines from the sources you pick. The same story from several outlets is shown once.
SportsLive scores, tables, line-ups and stats for football, hockey, basketball, UFC, Formula 1, tennis, esports and more.
Stocks and cryptoYour own list with prices, the change today and a small chart per line.
Twitch and YouTubeWho is live among the channels you follow, and the newest videos. Watch inside the page.
Reddit and TelegramThe subreddits and public channels you choose, with comments one click away.
MailHow many unread emails you have in Gmail, and from whom.
Weather and radioThe forecast for your city, and 70+ radio stations with the song that is playing, plus any station you add yourself.
PodcastsThe newest episodes of the shows you follow, the popular ones of your country, and a search of every podcast. They play right on the page.
Notes, calendar, timersNotes and to-dos, a calendar with holidays and birthdays, timers, countdowns and a stopwatch.

Scores, prices, streams and headlines update by themselves while the tab is open. A full list is on the features page.

Who it is for

  • People who follow several things at once (news, a few teams, the markets, some streamers) and want one place to see what has changed.
  • People who want a home page or new tab page they have built themselves, with only the sources they chose.
  • People who keep a tab open all day on a second screen.

How it works

  1. Open the page. Without an account you see a preview with sample content. Weather, sports, TV shows, podcasts, radio, the search bar, notes, the calendar, timers and the calculator already work, with examples filled in. A short welcome window shows you around, and you can try Edit before you sign up.
  2. Create a free account with an email address and a password. A short guide asks your country and what you are into, starts your page with good sources for it, and helps you pick your boxes. Every step can be skipped and done later.
  3. Make it yours. Switch boxes on and off, drag them where you want them, resize them, and choose colors, text size and a wallpaper.
  4. Use it anywhere. Your setup is saved to your account and follows you to any computer or phone where you sign in.

You choose the sources

OmniTree does not decide what you should read. There is no feed chosen for you and nothing is ranked to keep you scrolling: you add the news sites, channels, teams and symbols yourself, and the page shows them in the order they were published. The setup guide can start you off with a few well-known sources that fit what you like; you keep, change or remove them as you wish.

Connecting your own accounts

Some boxes show things from your own accounts. These are optional, and built so that OmniTree never sees your passwords:

  • Twitch: you log in on Twitch's own page and approve one permission, to read the list of channels you follow.
  • Gmail: you paste a small script into your own Google account. It answers with your unread count and the latest subjects, and only to a secret code that you hold. It cannot delete or move mail.
  • YouTube, Reddit, Telegram, news: no login at all. You only say which public channels and sites you want.

Step-by-step instructions are in Help. What is stored, and why, is in the privacy policy.

What it costs

OmniTree is free to use and has no ads. It is a small independent project run from Estonia. If that ever changes, it will be said openly on this site first.

Feedback

Found a bug or have an idea? On the start page, open the cogwheel menu and choose Feedback. It works without an account.
