Account & privacy

Looking for one setting? Open Settings and type in the search field at the top of its menu. For everything the search bar can do, type help in it.

Account & setup guide

Try it first

Before you sign in, the page is a working preview: sample news, posts, videos, stocks and mail; real sports, TV shows and podcasts; and example notes, calendar events and notifications. Every box and button can be tried.

  • The first time, a short welcome window explains the page. The ? next to Create free account opens it again.
  • Create an account from the preview and the layout you made, and the TV shows and podcasts in it, come with you. The example notes, events and notifications do not.

Create an account

  1. Click the person icon (the last of the small icons in the menu box).
  2. Choose Create account and enter your email and a password (at least 8 characters) twice.
  3. Open the confirmation email and click its link.
  4. Sign in.

The setup guide

The first time you sign in, a short guide sets up your page:

  1. Your country: a guess from your device’s time zone. Confirm or change it.
  2. What you are into: News, Football, Other sports, Investing, Gaming, Tech, Music, Podcasts.
  3. Your boxes: your interests tick the ones that fit. Untick or add any.
  4. Your city, for the weather and the clock.
  5. One page per box that needs setting up: news, Telegram, Reddit, stocks, TV shows, podcasts, Twitch, YouTube and Gmail.

Your interests fill the boxes with good sources: national news and your country’s subreddit, international news, official Telegram channels of news outlets, your country’s football league and the most popular podcasts of your country. Every step can be skipped and changed later.

  • Run the guide again: Settings → Layout & boxes → Run the setup guide again.
  • Add an interest without starting over: Settings → Layout & boxes → Add interests. It only adds sources, it never removes any.
  • Everything you set up is saved to your account and is the same on every device.

Finding your way

  • Settings: the cogwheel icon → Settings. Its search field finds any option.
  • Help: the cogwheel icon → Help, or press ?.
  • The search bar: type help in it.

Arranging the page

Choose Edit in the cogwheel menu (or press E). Nothing moves until you do something: each box gets a dashed outline and a small ⠿ tab on its top edge, and a bar with Undo, Cancel and Done appears at the bottom.

A box’s buttons

Click a box for its buttons. They also show the box’s size and text size.

  • ◀ ▶ move it one column; ▲ ▼ move it up or down.
  • W− W+ make it narrower or wider.
  • H− H+ show fewer or more rows of a list (down to one row).
  • T− T+ change that box’s own text size.
  • Hide switches the box off.

Dragging

  • Resize: drag the edge or corner of the selected box. The 12 columns appear, the width snaps to them and the height to whole rows, and the other boxes make room.
  • Move: drag the ⠿ tab. Its place stays open as an outlined gap so you see where it lands. Esc puts it back.
  • Keyboard: the arrows move the selected box, Shift + arrows resize it.
  • Bookmarks bar: drag a bookmark or folder to another place in the bar, and the bar’s ⠿ tab sideways to put the whole bar left, center or right. Settings → Bookmarks shows the same order and position.

Finishing

  • Done keeps the changes, Cancel puts everything back, Undo steps back one change.
  • Hidden boxes come back from the chips in the bar, or in Settings → Layout & boxes. A hidden box is not loaded.
  • On a list, a row’s ☆ and ✕ show when you point at it (and on every row of the selected box): ☆ makes it a favorite, ✕ hides that streamer, source or stock. Bring hidden ones back in Settings → Layout & boxes → Hidden items.

Phone and computer

  • A phone has its own layout: list lengths, text sizes and the order you change on a phone apply to phones only. Whatever you never changed on a phone follows the computer.
  • The bar says which layout you are editing.
  • On a phone the boxes sit in one column, so only ▲ ▼, the height and the text size are offered, and the bars sit at the bottom within reach of your thumb. Hold the ⠿ tab to move a box.
  • Hidden boxes are the same on every device.

Reloading and problems

  • Every list box has a ↻ button to reload it.
  • A small ! in a box means something could not be loaded. Click it for what failed, why, since when, and what you can do.

Colors & wallpaper

Appearance (Settings → Appearance)

  • Light or dark theme (or press T), and your accent color.
  • The colors of the background, the boxes, the text and the dim text (times, sources, notes), and how see-through the boxes are.
  • The text size for the whole page. A phone has its own: changing it on a phone leaves the computer as it is. One box’s own text size: Edit → T− T+.
  • The glow behind the page, the shadows, and rounded or square corners everywhere (boxes, menus, windows and buttons).
  • Signal colors per box: the green and red in Stocks, the live dot in Twitch, the live red and icons in Sports, each with a strength slider to tone it down.

Wallpaper (Settings → Wallpaper)

  1. Press Choose image.
  2. Choose how it fits: Fill screen (may crop), Fit whole image, or Original size.
  3. Press Reposition and drag the screen to move the image anywhere, also partly off the screen (handy for a picture with a frame around it). Zoom with the mouse wheel, two fingers, the slider or − +. Press Done. Reset position puts it back in the middle at its normal size.

Where the image does not reach, the page’s own background color shows.

Your wallpaper follows your account to your other devices.

Date, time & world clocks

All in Settings → Date & time:

  • Tick what the top of the page shows: day name, date, year, time, location, the weekday in front of the date, and seconds.
  • Write it the European or the American way, and choose a 24- or 12-hour clock.
  • Set the font, size and color of the day name, and the color of the next-event line under the date.
  • Location: press Change to set the city for the date and time (separate from the weather box).
  • World clocks: add up to five cities, shown in a small box when you point at the date, or on the page under it.

Weather

The weather box shows the weather now; click it for hour by hour and the coming days. A ! means a weather warning (strong wind, heavy rain or snow, frost, heat): point at it or open the box to read it.

Your city

Until you choose one, the city is a guess from your device’s time zone: Swedish time gives Stockholm, Estonian time Tallinn. No permission is asked, and neither your position nor your IP address is used, so it can be the wrong city in the right country. The setup guide asks you to confirm it.

There are two separate cities; changing one does not change the other:

  1. The weather box: click it, type a city, click a result, then press Use for the tile. Settings → Weather switches between °C and °F; by default the US gets °F, mph and inches. In the weather window, click a day of the 7 to see it hour by hour.
  2. The date and time at the top: Settings → Date & time → Change, type a city and click Use.

In the weather window you can also save more cities and switch between them.

News & Telegram

Discover news sites

The compass button at the top of the News box finds sources for you:

  • News sites: the main news sites of a country. Pick the country in the list beside the search field; World (English) has the big international ones.
  • Google News: Google News’s top stories and sections (World, Business, Sport…) in that country’s language, each gathered from many sites.
  • Search: type a site’s name, a topic or a web address. It looks in a public directory of news feeds and shows each one’s website, language, followers and when it last published (feeds quiet for a month are dimmed). A typed web address is offered as well: any news site can be followed through Google News.
  • Click a name (▸) to see its five newest headlines first, fetched just as the box will fetch them. + adds it, ✓ means you have it (click to remove).
  • What you add is a normal source: the reader, Languages shown and the merging of the same story from several sources work for it as for the others. If its language is one Languages shown leaves out, Discover says so. Shortcut: H.

Adding and removing sources (Settings → News, Telegram & Reddit)

  • News: give a name and a feed address (for example https://feeds.bbci.co.uk/news/world/rss.xml) or just a website (reuters.com), and press Add. A feed address is the most reliable; with only a website the page finds the news through search services. Up to 15. Start the name with the outlet’s name: sources whose names start with the same word (“BBC World” and “BBC Sport”) are treated as one outlet when the same story is grouped.
  • Telegram: type a channel name (ClashReport) or paste a t.me link, and press Add. Only public channels work.
  • ✕ removes a source; Back to default sources starts over. The list shows when each source last delivered, and why one was skipped.
  • The funnel button (Sources) at the top of each box hides a source for a while without deleting it.

Reading the box

  • A story you have not seen has an accent bar, and the count at the top counts them. Click the count to mark everything seen. Stories you opened are dimmed.
  • The same story from several outlets is one row with a count, also when one report is in Swedish and another in English. Three or more outlets within 3 hours make it hot. A story stays where it was first reported; another source joining does not mark it new again.
  • Point at a row for its picture and summary; click it to read it.
  • Highlight words mark headlines and tell you when a new one appears; mute words hide every headline that contains them.
  • With several channels, each Telegram post starts with its channel’s name. Channel names in Telegram posts turns that off, and Source names after headlines does the same for News and Reddit.

Search

The ⌕ button at the top of News, Telegram and Reddit opens a search field.

  • It looks through everything the box has loaded: the text and the source name. Every word must be there, in any order; capitals and accents do not matter.
  • A moment later (or at once with Enter) it looks further back: the last 30 days of your news sites, the whole history of your Telegram channels, and the last year of your subreddits. What it finds joins the list, newest first.
  • Press ⌕ again to close the search.

The reader

Click a post and it opens in a window with the box’s other posts above and below it, each in full with its picture or video, so you scroll from one to the next.

  • One post at a time: a long post scrolls inside the window and stops at its end; a couple more wheel clicks move on to the next.
  • News: the full article is fetched from the site and shown under the picture. Where a site does not allow it (a paywall, some Google News links) the summary stays with a note, and Read the full article opens the site.
  • Catching up: the small list button at the top of the box (before ⌕) opens the reader at the oldest post you have not seen, under a New line, and you scroll down to the newest. A post counts as read once you reach it.
  • Keys: ↓ ↑ (or J K) go one post on or back; Esc closes. The button beside Close (↓ Newer or ↓ Older; on a phone ↓ New or ↓ Old) turns the order around. Aa beside it changes the reader’s font (easy-to-read ones such as Atkinson Hyperlegible), text size and line spacing; only the posts change, and this device remembers it. Beside it, 7 new counts the unread posts still ahead of you and counts down as you read; 12/48 shows where you are in the list.
  • Videos do not start by themselves, and stop when you scroll away.
  • Telegram: Load older posts at the old end. Reddit: Top comments under a post.
  • On a phone the reader fills the screen and you scroll with your thumb. On a phone, the back button (or back gesture) closes the open window or Settings instead of leaving the page.

Reading settings (Settings → News, Telegram & Reddit)

  • Clicking a post opens the reader, or just that post.
  • Scrolling in the reader moves one post at a time, or freely.
  • More reader options: which way scrolling down goes after a click and from the catch-up button; the reader’s height (90, 75, 60 or 50% of the screen; always full on a phone); Wheel clicks needed to go to the next post (1 to 6); Pause the wheel after arriving at a post, so a row of short posts cannot fly past. The last two only count with one post at a time.
  • Languages shown in News: headlines in other languages are left out (nothing is translated).
  • Headlines shown and Telegram posts shown: the length of the lists. The button at the end shows 20 more; for Telegram it also loads older posts.
  • When you come back to the page: unread posts stay new, or what was on screen counts as seen.

Reddit

  • Add or remove subreddits in Settings → News, Telegram & Reddit (up to 12).
  • In the box, choose Best, Hot, New, Top or Rising. The funnel (Sources) hides a subreddit for a while.
  • Point at a post for a preview; click it for the text, the links and the top comments. How many comments (3 to 20, or none) is set under Top comments under a Reddit post.
  • Reddit gives a flat list without votes; the button in the window opens the whole discussion on Reddit.

Search, the reader and catching up work as in News & Telegram.

Sports

Choose your leagues in Settings → Sports. ESPN supplies football (leagues and cups from more than twenty countries, the World Cup, the European Championship, the Nations League), NBA, college basketball and football, NHL, NFL, MLB, rugby, cricket, Australian football, tennis, Formula 1, IndyCar, NASCAR, golf and UFC. SHL (Swedish hockey) comes from shl.se, and Counter-Strike 2, League of Legends and Dota 2 from bo3.gg; those four need you to be signed in.

The box

  • Pick a sport and a view: Live & today, Upcoming, Results, Favorites, and ★ your favorite sport (set in Settings → Sports: everything on now, today and coming up in that sport).
  • Point at a game for a short summary; click it for its window. While a game is on, its window updates by itself: every 20 seconds (SHL and esports every minute). ‹ › beside Close (or ← →) go to the previous or next game in the box, or the previous or next fight on a UFC card.
  • Follow one game: the bell beside ‹ › in a game’s window (also a UFC fight or a whole UFC card, a tennis match, a race weekend or a golf tournament). You are told only the big moments for that sport: football the kick-off, every goal, extra time or penalties and full time; ice hockey every goal, overtime and the final; esports every map; basketball, American football, rugby and Australian football the half-time score, overtime and the final; baseball and cricket the start and the final; a fight how it ended, and a whole card each fight’s start and outcome; a tennis match each set (a whole tournament has too many matches, so you follow one match); a race weekend when qualifying, a sprint and the race start and who took pole or won (not practice); golf the winner. It stops by itself when the game is over, and keeps checking while the page is in a background tab; to be told while you are in another tab, switch on desktop notifications (the bubble under the bell offers it on a computer). If a followed game ended while the page was closed, you get its result when you open it. A small bell marks the games you follow in the box.
  • Results goes back in time, newest first with a heading for each day: team leagues to the start of the month (in its first week, the month before too); UFC, Formula 1 and golf four weeks; tennis, SHL and esports about a week. It lists the 120 newest; pick one sport to see further back.
  • Search: ⌕ finds a team, player, fighter, league or event in everything loaded for your leagues. Several words narrow it down (ufc allen). Press ⌕ again to close.

A game’s window

What it shows depends on the sport and on what the source has:

  • Team sports: goals and cards with minute and player, the score of each period, match and team stats, the best player lines of each team, line-ups, both sides’ last five results, and the league table with both teams highlighted. Before the game: the odds and who is unavailable.
  • Rugby: every try, conversion and penalty with minute and scorer (T, C, P, DG), the cards, line-ups and the table with bonus points. Cricket: the result, batting and bowling figures and the table with net run rate. Australian football: the goals, each quarter, team stats, the players with the most disposals and the ladder.
  • Formula 1: the order of every session that has run (click a session), and the driver and team standings. Golf: the leaderboard round by round. IndyCar and NASCAR: the race order and driver standings.
  • Tennis: every match as a small scoreboard, one line per player with the games of each set and the winner ticked, and the world ranking below.
  • SHL: the goals with assists, the score of each period, team stats, the best skaters and the goalies.
  • Esports: both teams’ last five, world ranking and recent meetings, and every map or game with its score. Counter-Strike 2 adds the players’ kills, deaths, assists and rating; Dota 2 a scoreboard for each game with heroes and net worth. Click a team for its roster, career numbers, next matches, last results and titles, and a player in the roster for the player. Watch buttons dock the Twitch stream showing the match.

Teams and players

Team and player names can be clicked wherever they appear: in game windows, league tables, rankings, tennis matches and Formula 1 and golf results. They turn your accent color when you point at them.

  • A team: its league position, record and coach, the last five results as W/D/L, its results and coming games side by side, and the squad by position with number, age and nationality (! = injured). SHL teams also show the club’s history, the team’s figures (goals per game, power play, penalty kill) and their top scorers.
  • A player: team, position, number, age, birthplace, nationality, height and weight, and their statistics. Tennis players also show their matches in the tournaments that are loaded.
  • Click on to another team or player from there; ← Back goes back the same way.
  • UFC fighters and esports teams and players have windows of their own (below and above).

UFC

  • An upcoming event is one row. Click it for the fight card: a table with the fighters facing each other, the odds to win beside each name, and the weight class and status in their own columns.
  • While a card is on, every fight is its own row: the one in the cage first with round and clock, then what is next, then the results.
  • Click a fight for both fighters side by side, the fight’s numbers (strikes, takedowns, control time) and a tale of the tape. Point at a fighter for a small card; click the name for the fighter’s window.

Favorites

  • Press ☆ next to a team, fighter or player in a window. Favorites are marked ★ and listed first.
  • You are told when their game starts, at every goal (football and ice hockey) and at the final score.
  • Their games show in the calendar. Both can be switched off (Settings → Sports and Settings → Notifications).

Sports in your calendar

  • + Add to calendar (in the window of any game, card, race or tournament) puts that event in your calendar with a reminder 30 minutes before. Change or remove it with ✎ in the calendar.
  • + Every … event does the same for every coming event of that league, also the ones announced later, and keeps them at the right time when one is moved. An event you delete yourself is not put back.
  • Switch it off with the same button or in Settings → Sports → Events in your calendar; the events still to come leave the calendar again.
  • The reminders need Calendar reminders switched on in Settings → Notifications.

Stocks

Adding stocks (Settings → Stocks)

Stocks, funds, indexes and crypto can be added in five ways:

  1. Search by name: type a company name such as Tesla. A list shows the symbol, the full name and the exchange; press Add on the right one. A company is often on several exchanges, so pick the one you hold. If you know the symbol (NVDA or NASDAQ:NVDA), type it and press Add.
  2. Paste a list of symbols separated by commas, and press Import.
  3. Import a file: choose the CSV file from your broker or portfolio site. The page looks for a Symbol, Ticker or ISIN column and shows what it found, so you can untick anything first. Files with only ISIN codes work too; any that cannot be found are listed so you can add them by name. Only the symbols are read; amounts, prices and account numbers are not, and the file is not uploaded. Save an Excel file as CSV first.
  4. A Google Sheet: in Google Sheets press Share, set it to Anyone with the link (Viewer), copy the link and paste it into the link field. The sheet needs the same kind of column, with the names in its first row. For another tab of the sheet, open that tab before copying the link. It is read once, not followed.
  5. Your TradingView watchlist:
    1. Open any chart on tradingview.com while signed in. Your watchlist is the panel on the right.
    2. Click ••• at the top of that panel and choose Advanced view (or Share); turn link sharing on.
    3. Copy the link (it looks like https://www.tradingview.com/watchlists/12345678/), paste it into the link field and press Import.

The box

  • Each row shows the price, a small chart and the day’s change. Choose what the chart covers (today, the week, the month, or off) in Settings → Stocks. In a narrow box the price is cut short to make room.
  • Sort with the dropdown. Star or hide a stock in Edit mode. To take one off the list for good, press ✕ next to it in Settings → Stocks.
  • Point at a stock for its full name, the price with its currency and today’s change. Click a stock for its chart and recent news. The switch above the chart changes between a clean price line (point anywhere for the price; 1 day, 1 month, 3 months, 1 year, 5 years or all time) and the full chart with candles, volume and tools. Your choice is kept. ‹ › beside Close (or ← →) go to the previous or next stock in the box.
  • Big indices are drawn with TradingView’s own index price, as the official feeds cannot be shown outside TradingView. For the Nasdaq Composite that is the Nasdaq 100, as the window says.

Market hours (the clock button)

  • The clock has the box’s “up” color while the market most of your stocks are on is open, and its “down” color while it is closed.
  • Point at it for your main markets; click it for all of them: how many of your stocks are on each, open or closed, how long until it opens or closes, and at what time on your own clock. Click a market to see which of your stocks belong to it.
  • Crypto is left out (it never closes). Currencies and commodities (oil, gold) trade almost around the clock from Sunday evening to Friday evening.
  • The market comes from the symbol (NASDAQ:NVDA, SAAB-B.ST); a symbol without an exchange counts as a US stock. Main public holidays are counted, shortened trading days are not.

Alerts

  • Price alerts: the bell button next to the clock opens one sentence: Alert me when [stock] is [more than / less than / reaches] [price]. The price starts at today’s. You get one notification and the alert switches itself off; tick it to set it again, change it right in the list, or remove it with ✕. Set a price alert in a stock’s window does the same. Up to 30, checked about once a minute while the page is open.
  • Big moves: a notification when a stock moves fast (within about half an hour) or far in a day. Set the percentages in Settings → Notifications.

TV shows

The box is off at first: switch it on in Settings → Layout & boxes. Show information comes from TVmaze.com; films are not included. Times are in your own time zone.

The box: your shows

  • Coming up lists the next episode of each show you follow, soonest first, under a heading per day. Point at a row for a small card about the show.
  • Just aired lists the latest episode of each, newest first.
  • Click a row for the show: what it is about, where it runs, its rating, coming and latest episodes, and a button to follow it or stop.
  • The next and the latest episode of each show sit on their day in the calendar (switch off in Settings → TV shows), and you can be told when a new one airs (Settings → Notifications).
  • The shows you follow, each with ✕, are listed in Settings → TV shows. Up to 60.

Discover (the compass button)

  • Shows what airs today, with chips for tomorrow and the five days after: the best-known shows on television in the chosen country and on streaming services that release worldwide (Netflix, Max, Prime Video…), best-known first in each day. Where TVmaze lists little for your country, other worldwide releases (such as anime) fill the day below them, under a heading of their own. Several episodes of one show on one day are one row (S01E30–E34).
  • The list next to the search field chooses the country (also in Settings → TV shows). TVmaze has full listings for the US and the UK; for most other countries the streaming part is what fills the list.
  • Type in the field to search for any show.
  • Click a show for its page in the same window; ← Back returns. ☆ follows a show, ★ means you follow it.
  • Shortcut: V.

Twitch

Connecting

  1. Open Settings → Twitch and press Connect.
  2. Log in on Twitch if asked and press Authorize. The page only reads who you follow; it cannot chat, follow or change anything.

The box

  • Who you follow that is live, with game and viewers. Sort it or pick a category with the dropdowns.
  • Point at a channel for a preview, the title and how long it has been live; click it to watch inside the page.
  • ★ a streamer in Edit mode to be told when they go live. Starred streamers who are offline are listed dimmed under the live ones, with when they were last live (switch off in Settings → Twitch).

Discover (the compass button)

  • Starts with the biggest live streams right now. Pick a category for its ten biggest streams; Show 10 more for the next ten, All categories and More categories for further ones.
  • Type in the field to search for a channel or a game, and choose a language to see only streams in it.
  • Click a stream to watch it; the player has a Discover button to go back.
  • ☆ puts a channel on your own list without following it on Twitch: it then shows in the box when live, among your ★ streamers when not, and you are told when it goes live.
  • Discover needs your Twitch account connected. Each answer is kept for a minute. Shortcut: D.

Chat asks you to log in, or ads despite Turbo or a sub?

The player inside the page is a Twitch “embed”. Your browser treats it as a third-party site and hides your twitch.tv login from it, so Twitch thinks you are logged out. This is a browser setting; Connect does not change it. Two fixes:

  1. Pop out (works everywhere): press Pop out on a stream, or turn on Settings → Twitch → Open streams in a pop-out window. The pop-out is twitch.tv itself, so your login, Turbo and subs work. Allow pop-ups for this site if the browser asks.
  2. Let the embed see your login by allowing third-party cookies for Twitch only. Quickest in Chrome and Edge: click the icon at the left end of the address bar on this page and allow third-party cookies. Or in the browser’s settings:
    • Chrome: Settings → Privacy and security → Third-party cookies → Sites allowed to use third-party cookies → Add → [*.]twitch.tv.
    • Edge: Settings → Cookies and site permissions → Manage and delete cookies → Allow → Add [*.]twitch.tv (and set Tracking prevention to Balanced).
    • Firefox: Settings → Privacy & Security → Cookies and Site Data → Manage Exceptions → add https://www.twitch.tv and https://player.twitch.tv as Allow.
    • Brave: the lion icon on this site → Advanced → Cookies: Allow all.
    • Safari cannot allow this per site; use Pop out.
    Then be logged in at twitch.tv in the same browser and reload this page.

YouTube

The box shows new videos from the channels you choose: your subscriptions (from a file, once) and channels you add with Discover. Click a video to watch it inside the page; it can be docked like a Twitch stream.

Discover (the compass button)

  • Starts with the most viewed videos of today. The chips switch to Top this week, Live now (sorted by viewers) and topics such as Music, Gaming, News, Sports and Podcasts (most viewed this week); All topics shows the rest.
  • Type in the field to search for any video, channel or playlist; matching channels and playlists come first. Paste a YouTube link to play it (a playlist link opens the playlist).
  • Each row shows the title, the channel, how long ago and how long, and the views (for live: how many are watching). Show 20 more loads the next ones.
  • Click a video to watch it; the player has a Discover button to go back. Click a channel’s name for its newest videos, with a Playlists tab for its playlists; ← Back returns step by step.
  • ☆ puts a channel in your YouTube box, next to your subscriptions; ★ takes it out again. This works without the file below.
  • Discover needs you to be signed in. Each answer is kept for 10 minutes. Shortcut: Y.

Playlists

  • Click a playlist for its videos. ▶ Play all plays it from the start and goes on through it by itself; clicking one of its videos starts there. Dock keeps it playing beside the page, with the rest of the playlist in Up next (see Docking).
  • ☆ on a playlist saves it: the ★ Saved chip, first in Discover, lists your saved playlists (up to 50). ★ forgets one.
  • Only public playlists can be shown; your own private ones (Liked videos, Watch later) are not readable by websites.

Your subscriptions

YouTube does not let a website read your subscriptions or your recommendations, so you hand the page a small file, once. It takes about five minutes and the file only holds channel names.

  1. Go to takeout.google.com, signed in to your YouTube account.
  2. Click Deselect all near the top.
  3. Scroll down to YouTube and YouTube Music and tick it.
  4. On the same row, click All YouTube data included; untick everything, then tick only subscriptions.
  5. Click Next step, then Create export.
  6. Google emails you a download link, usually within minutes. Download the file.
  7. Open it and find subscriptions.csv (in the “YouTube and YouTube Music” folder).

Mail (Gmail)

The Mail box shows how many unread emails you have in up to two Gmail inboxes, without giving this site your Google password. It needs a small piece of free code in your own Google account, once per inbox. It is all copy and paste: follow the steps in order.

Is this safe? Yes. This site never gets your password and cannot sign in as you. What connects an inbox (your Gmail script’s address and token) is kept on the server, encrypted, in a place no browser can read; it is never in your settings, your browser or a backup file. The site can only see your unread mail: sender, subject and first line. It cannot delete or move mail, or write to anyone else. To stop it at any time, press Remove in Settings → Mail, delete the project at script.google.com, or remove its access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Setting it up

  1. Go to script.google.com, signed in as the Gmail account you want to show (check the top right corner).
  2. Click New project (top left).
  3. Click inside the box with sample code, press Ctrl+A (Cmd+A on a Mac) and Delete.
  4. Copy the code below and paste it into the empty box (Ctrl+V or Cmd+V).
// Paste this whole file into your Apps Script project (replace what is there), then
// Deploy > Manage deployments > pencil > Version: New version > Deploy.
// Version 2: the site sends the token in the body of the request (POST), never in the address.
var VERSION = 2;

function out(text) {
  return ContentService.createTextOutput(text).setMimeType(ContentService.MimeType.JSON);
}

// Opened in a browser (nothing after /exec): shows your token once. The site also asks which version this is (?ping=1).
function doGet(e) {
  try {
    var q = (e && e.parameter) || {};
    if (q.ping) return out(JSON.stringify({ v: VERSION }));
    if (q.t) return out(JSON.stringify({ error: "This script takes the token only in the body of a request. Update the site or the script." }));
    var p = PropertiesService.getScriptProperties();
    if (p.getProperty("T")) return out(JSON.stringify({ error: "Already set up. To get a new token, run resetToken in the editor, then open this address again." }));
    var token = Utilities.getUuid();
    p.setProperty("T", token);
    return out(JSON.stringify({ setup: "Copy this token into the site (Settings, Mail). It is shown only once.", token: token }));
  } catch (err) {
    return out(JSON.stringify({ error: String(err && err.message ? err.message : err) }));
  }
}

// What the site asks, with the token in the body: { op: "read" } for your unread mail, { op: "send" } to email you a notification.
function doPost(e) {
  try {
    var token = PropertiesService.getScriptProperties().getProperty("T");
    var d = JSON.parse(e.postData.contents);
    if (!token || d.t !== token) return out(JSON.stringify({ error: "Wrong token" }));
    if (d.op === "read") return out(read(parseInt(d.since, 10) || 0));
    if (d.op === "send") {
      // only ever to this same Gmail address
      MailApp.sendEmail(Session.getEffectiveUser().getEmail(), String(d.subject || "OmniTree").slice(0, 200), String(d.body || "").slice(0, 8000));
      return out(JSON.stringify({ sent: true }));
    }
    return out(JSON.stringify({ error: "Unknown request" }));
  } catch (err) {
    return out(JSON.stringify({ error: String(err && err.message ? err.message : err) }));
  }
}

// since: seconds; the moment you pressed "Mark read" on the site
function read(since) {
  var cache = CacheService.getScriptCache();
  var key = "m" + since;
  var hit = cache.get(key);
  if (hit) return hit;
  var email = Session.getEffectiveUser().getEmail();
  var items = GmailApp.search("in:inbox is:unread", 0, 20).map(function (t) {
    var msgs = t.getMessages();
    var m = msgs[msgs.length - 1];
    return {
      from: m.getFrom(),
      subject: m.getSubject(),
      snippet: m.getPlainBody().replace(/\s+/g, " ").slice(0, 200),
      ts: m.getDate().getTime(),
      link: "https://mail.google.com/mail/?authuser=" + encodeURIComponent(email) + "#inbox/" + t.getId()
    };
  }).sort(function (a, b) { return b.ts - a.ts; });
  var fresh = since ? GmailApp.search("in:inbox is:unread after:" + since, 0, 30).length : null;
  var text = JSON.stringify({ email: email, unread: GmailApp.getInboxUnreadCount(), fresh: fresh, items: items });
  cache.put(key, text, 120);
  return text;
}

// Run this once from the editor only if you lose the token and need a new one.
function resetToken() {
  PropertiesService.getScriptProperties().deleteProperty("T");
}
  1. Click the untitled project name at the top, rename it (for example “Mail for my start page”) and press Enter.
  2. Press Ctrl+S (Cmd+S) to save.
  3. Click the blue Deploy button (top right) → New deployment.
  4. Click the gear next to “Select type” and choose Web app.
  5. Set Execute as to Me and Who has access to Anyone (not “Anyone with a Google account”).
  6. Click Deploy.
  7. Google asks you to authorize the script: click Authorize access and choose your account. If it warns that the app is not verified, click Advanced → Go to … (unsafe) → Allow. This is normal for your own private script.
  8. Copy the Web app URL (it ends in /exec).
  9. Open that address in a new tab. It shows a token, a long code in quotes. Copy it now; it is shown only once. (If you lose it, the code itself explains how to reset it.)
  10. Here, open Settings → Mail, press Connect Gmail at an inbox, fill in a name (“Personal”), the web app address and the token, and press Save.
  11. Within a minute your unread count appears.

For a second Gmail inbox, repeat the steps while signed in to that Gmail account.

Using it

  • Point at a mailbox for the latest unread subjects; click it for the list.
  • Mark read counts only mail that arrives from now on (on this site only), and clears that inbox's new-mail notifications from the bell, on all your devices.
  • In Settings → Mail each inbox shows its name (change it right there), what it is, and Replace and Remove. The address and token cannot be shown again once saved, like a password: to change them, press Replace.
  • Emails from this page (notifications by email, and the “new device signed in” warning) are sent by your Gmail script to its own address. Set the script up a while ago? Replace its code with the code above and deploy again (Deploy → Manage deployments → pencil → Version: New version → Deploy); the address and token stay the same. The current version takes its token only in the body of each request, never in a web address; while an inbox still uses an older one, Settings → Mail says so and shows how to update.

Your events

Taking your events elsewhere

Heard something and saw nothing?

On all your devices

Pressing “x new”, opening a post, opening the bell, removing a notification and clearing the history are saved to your account: the phone shows the same within a minute, and nothing is announced twice.

While you are away

While the page’s tab is in the background (another tab is selected, or the window is minimized), everything still updates, more calmly the longer you are away: after 10 minutes, then 15, then 30, then every hour. The boxes, the bell and the count in the tab title stay current.

When you come back and the last update is more than 15 minutes old, everything is fetched at once and gaps are filled: Telegram fetches back to the posts it had (up to about 100 per channel), and News and Reddit (sorted by New) keep what they had. Nothing posted while you were away is skipped, and there is no need to reload the page.

Summary

One small window with what happened lately. Open it from the cogwheel menu or with B. Set it up in Settings → Summary:

  • When it opens by itself: never, on the first visit of the day, when you come back after being away, or both. You choose how long “away” is (30 minutes to 8 hours).
  • How far back it looks: the last 6, 12 or 24 hours, or 2 days.
  • What is in it: weather and warnings, today in your calendar, top stories, Telegram, Reddit, biggest stock movers, mail, who is live, ★ streamers who were live while you were away, TV episodes, new podcast episodes, sports results and sports coming up. Tick the parts you want and order them with ▲ ▼. A part with nothing to show is left out.
  • Choose… on a part picks which sources, channels, subreddits or sports it uses, or keeps it to your ★ ones. New sources you add later are included.
  • How it looks: a list, cards side by side, or compact, and how many items each part shows (3 to 15).

Account & privacy

Sign-in & security (Settings → Sign-in & security)

  • Set a new password (typed twice). Forgot it? Click the person icon → Forgot password? and a reset link is emailed to you.
  • Every device and browser signed in to your account right now, the latest sign-ins, and Sign out everywhere else.
  • You can be emailed the first time a new browser signs in (needs an inbox under Mail).

Your data (Settings → Your data)

  • Download backup saves all settings, sources, notes and layout as a file; Restore from file brings it back. Your mail connections are not in it; they stay on the server with your account.
  • Delete my account removes the account and everything saved in it, for good.

Privacy

  • Everything you set up is private to your account; nobody else using the site can see it. It follows you to every device: settings, sources, bookmarks, notes, calendar, timers, world clocks, and what you have seen and opened. Changes reach your other devices within a minute, and two devices changing different things never overwrite each other.
  • When another account signs in on the same browser, the previous account’s things are cleared from it first.
  • To run the site, it counts how long the page is open, how often each kind of button is used, and how often each part of the page asks the server for data; never what you read, type, search or save. Each visit is noted with its IP address, country and browser, and while the page is open and in view, when it was last in use; kept for 90 days and seen only by the site owner.
  • Text you translate goes to the MyMemory translation service; radio plays straight from the stations’ servers; a bookmark with “the site’s own icon” fetches it from that site.
