Privacy policy

Last updated: 24 October 2026

This page says what OmniTree stores about you, why, for how long, and what you can do about it. It is written to be read, not to hide things.

In short

  • You can use the preview without an account. An account needs only an email address and a password.
  • Every visit is logged with its IP address for 90 days, for security and to count visitors.
  • What you set up and write (sources, layout, notes, to-dos, calendar) is stored so that it follows you between devices.
  • Nothing is sold, there are no ads and no advertising or analytics companies are involved.
  • You can download your data and delete your account yourself, at any time, in Settings.

Who runs OmniTree

OmniTree (omnitr.ee) is run by its operator, based in Estonia, who is the data controller for the personal data described here. Because the operator is in the European Union, the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) applies. You reach the operator through the form at the bottom of this page.

When you visit, with or without an account

Each time the start page is opened, one line is saved:

Reloading within 30 minutes does not add a new line. While the page stays open and in view, the line also keeps when it was last in use (updated every 5 minutes), to see how long visits last; nothing about what you do on the page is part of it. These lines are deleted after 90 days.

Without an account, everything you set up stays in your own browser (its local storage) and is not sent to OmniTree.

Your starting city. Until you choose a city, the weather and the clock use the main city of the time zone your device is set to (for example Stockholm for Swedish time). This is worked out in your own browser from its time zone setting. Your position is not requested, no permission is asked for, and your IP address is not used to locate you. To get the forecast, the name of that city is then sent to the weather service, exactly as when you pick a city yourself.

When you create an account

  • Email address: to sign you in and to send the confirmation and password-reset emails. It is not used for newsletters or marketing.
  • Password: stored only as a scrambled value (a hash) by the sign-in service. The operator cannot see your password.
  • Sign-in records: when the account was created, when you last signed in, and the devices that are signed in right now with their IP address and browser. The sign-in service also logs each sign-up, sign-in, sign-out and password reset with its IP address; the operator sees these for the last 30 days, for security and to see how the site is used. You can see your own under Settings → Sign-in & security, and sign other devices out there.

What you save in OmniTree

So that your page looks the same on every device, your setup is stored with your account:

  • Your settings and layout: which boxes are on, where they are, colors, text size, shortcuts, how the date and time are shown, your world-clock cities, what your summary shows, and the price alerts you set.
  • Your bookmarks: the names and addresses of the links on your bookmarks bar.
  • Your sources: news sites, Telegram channels, subreddits, YouTube channels, stock symbols, sports leagues, favorite teams and players, the TV shows you follow, the podcasts you follow, cities for the weather, radio stations (also the ones you add yourself), and the country you chose in the setup guide.
  • What you write: notes, to-dos, calendar events (also the ones you import from a calendar file), birthdays, timers and alarms.
  • The addresses of calendars you follow and of the web pages you show in web page boxes. A calendar’s “secret address” gives access to that calendar, so it is stored like the other connection details below and used only to read the calendar.
  • Your read-later list: the title, source and address of each headline, post or video you put aside.
  • What you have already seen: up to when each box counts as read, which posts you opened, which notifications were already given and when you cleared them, and for podcasts where you stopped in an episode and which episodes you played. This is what lets your phone and your computer agree. Posts and notifications are kept as short fingerprints (a few letters and digits each), not as their addresses or texts.
  • Your wallpaper picture, if you upload one.
  • For connected accounts: the access code Twitch gives the page, and the address and secret code of your own Gmail script. The Gmail connections are kept only on the server, encrypted, where no browser can read them (see Connected accounts).

Each account can only read and change its own data; this is enforced in the database itself. The operator has technical access to the database, as anyone running a service does, but does not read what you have written. The administration page only shows counts (for example "4 notes"), never the contents.

How the site is used

While you are signed in, three things are counted per day:

  • how long the page was open and visible,
  • how many times each kind of button was pressed, for example "News: opened a headline" or "Sports: starred a team", and
  • how many times each part of the page asked OmniTree's server for data, for example "News headlines" or "YouTube search". This keeps the service within the limits of its hosting. Requests made without an account are counted together, not per person.

What you read, type, search for or save is never recorded this way. The counts are used to see which parts of the site are worth improving and to keep the service healthy.

If you send feedback or a privacy request, its text is stored together with your IP address, your account if you are signed in, the email address you give, and the technical details you choose to attach (site version, browser, window size, theme).

Connected accounts and sources

  • Twitch: you log in on Twitch's own page. OmniTree receives an access code that only allows reading the list of channels you follow. It never sees your Twitch password. Discover uses the same access code to ask Twitch for public streams, categories and the channel or game you search for; that goes from your browser straight to Twitch. You can remove the access in Settings → Twitch or on twitch.tv.
  • Gmail: you install a small script in your own Google account. OmniTree stores the script's address and its secret code on its server, encrypted, and asks the script for your unread count and the sender, subject and first line of the latest unread emails. These pass through OmniTree's server on the way to your browser and are not stored there. The script cannot delete or move mail. You can stop it at any time with Remove in Settings, Mail, or by deleting the script in your Google account.
  • News, Reddit, Telegram, YouTube, some sports and esports: OmniTree's server fetches these public sources for you. The list of sources you asked for is sent to the server for that purpose; the answers are kept for a few minutes to avoid fetching the same thing twice, not linked to you. YouTube Discover works the same way: what you search for is sent through the server to YouTube, without anything that identifies you.
  • Stocks: OmniTree's server fetches prices, charts and news for the symbols on your list from public market-data services. When you search for a company, the name you typed is sent the same way, and so are ISIN codes that need to be turned into symbols. These requests are made by the server, not linked to you, and the answers are kept briefly to avoid asking twice.
  • Importing stocks from a file: the file is read in your own browser and is not uploaded. Only the symbols (or ISIN codes) you choose to add are kept; amounts, prices and account numbers in the file are ignored.
  • Importing stocks from a Google Sheet: the link you paste is sent to OmniTree's server, which reads the sheet once from Google and passes it to your browser, where it is handled like a file. Neither the link nor the sheet is stored. It only works for sheets you have shared with “Anyone with the link”.
  • Calendars you follow: OmniTree’s server reads the calendar from the address you gave, every half hour while the page is open, and passes it to your browser. The events are kept in your browser only; they are not stored on the server and not saved with your account. An .ics file you import is read in your browser and not uploaded; the events you add from it become ordinary calendar events of yours.
  • Bookmarks you import: the export file of your browser is read in your own browser and not uploaded. Only the links and folders you tick are saved, like any other bookmark.
  • Web page boxes: a page you show in a box is loaded by your browser straight from that site, exactly as if you had opened it in a tab. That site sees your IP address and browser and may set its own cookies, under its own privacy policy. It cannot read anything from OmniTree. When you add an address while signed in, OmniTree’s server asks that site once whether it allows being shown in a box; nothing about you is sent with that question.
  • Email notifications: if you switch them on, they are sent by your own Gmail script to your own address.

Cookies and local storage

OmniTree sets no advertising or tracking cookies. It uses your browser's local storage to remember your settings and to keep you signed in; this is necessary for the page to work. Players embedded from Twitch, YouTube and TradingView may set cookies of their own when you use them.

How long data is kept

  • Visit log: 90 days.
  • Account, settings, what you saved, usage counts: until you delete your account. Deleting it removes them at once.
  • Feedback and privacy requests: until they have been dealt with, and no longer than two years.
  • Backups made by the hosting provider may hold deleted data for a short time before they are overwritten.

Why this is allowed (legal basis)

  • To provide the service you asked for (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)): your account, your settings and what you save.
  • Legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)): keeping the service secure, preventing abuse, and understanding how many people use it and which parts. You may object to this, see below.
  • Your consent (Article 6(1)(a)): connecting Twitch or Gmail, and anything you choose to send in a form. You can withdraw it by disconnecting.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you may ask to see the personal data held about you, have it corrected or deleted, have its use restricted, receive it in a portable form, and object to processing based on legitimate interests.

Most of this you can do yourself, immediately:

  • Get a copy: Settings → Your data → Backup downloads your whole setup as a file.
  • Correct: change anything in Settings, including your password.
  • Delete: Settings → Your data → Delete account removes the account and everything saved in it. This cannot be undone.

For anything else, use the form below. Requests are answered within one month. If you are not satisfied, you may complain to the data protection authority where you live, or to the Estonian Data Protection Inspectorate (Andmekaitse Inspektsioon, aki.ee).

Children

OmniTree is not meant for children under 13, and accounts should not be created by them.

Security

All traffic is encrypted (HTTPS). Passwords are stored only as hashes. Each account is walled off from the others in the database. No system is perfectly safe; if a breach affecting your data happens, you will be told as the law requires.

Changes

OmniTree is currently free and has no ads. That may change in the future, for example by adding a voluntary tip jar or other ways to cover its costs. If a change affects what data is collected or who receives it, this policy will be updated before it takes effect, with a new date at the top.

Contact and privacy requests

Use this form for questions about this policy or to exercise your rights. An email address is required so that you can be answered. If the request is about an account, write from, or give, the email address of that account.
